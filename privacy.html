Privacy

panic button privacy policy

Last updated 28 November 2026.

panic button runs on your own computer. There is no panic button server, account or analytics. The seller never receives your data.

What it accesses

  • Gmail: your mail settings only (filters, forwarding, POP and IMAP), through the gmail.settings.basic permission. It never reads, sends or deletes your messages.
  • Outlook.com: your mailbox settings (inbox rules and forwarding).
  • GitHub, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Slack: the security settings and sign-in access you connect it to.

Where your data goes

  • Sign-in tokens and a snapshot of your settings are kept in an encrypted vault on your computer. The key stays with you.
  • Your computer talks directly to Google, Microsoft, GitHub or Slack. Nothing passes through anyone else.
  • Nothing is sold, shared, or used for advertising or AI training.

What it does with it

It compares your settings with the snapshot you approved. If something risky appears, it asks you first; if you don't answer by the deadline, it removes what was added and signs intruders out. Everything it changes is listed in a report on your computer.

Removing access

Delete the vault file to remove everything panic button stored. You can also revoke its access at any time from your Google, Microsoft or GitHub account.

Google user data

panic button's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

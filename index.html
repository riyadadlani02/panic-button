One pressThree moves

Always in this order, starting with the account that can reset the others.

Cuts off access

A company account is suspended or blocked and signed out everywhere. On a personal account it shuts the ways mail can leave.

Cleans up

Removes what was added since you last approved the account: mail rules, forwarding addresses, keys, app access, delegates.

Hands you the rest

Some steps no tool is allowed to do, like changing your password. You get each one as a link, in order.

Or don'tPressAt all

Leave it on guard. Every few minutes it compares each account with the settings you approved. A new forwarding rule, a new key or a sign-in the provider flags, and it asks you first (a dialog on macOS). If nobody answers within 15 minutes, it acts on the risky changes (by default it presses the account) and tells you.

It decides from a fixed list of rules, with no AI model involved. Say "It was me" and the changes you were asked about become your new baseline. It presses an account at most once in 24 hours and never touches your protected admin.

What it will not claim

  • Access an intruder already holds can last up to an hour after it is revoked.
  • It cannot see someone who only reads your mail.
  • A personal account has no true lock. The intruder is out when you sign out everywhere and change the password.
  • It does not clean malware. Press from a device you trust.

What it cannot stop

  • Theft at a vendor you already connected to.
  • SIM swaps at your carrier.
  • Wallet and seed-phrase scams.
  • iCloud: it has no API.
  • Data copied in the first minutes, before any setting changes.

After you download

  1. Open the .dmg and drag panic button to Applications.
  2. Open it. macOS will refuse the first time, because the app is unsigned. Go to System Settings, Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway.
  3. The app walks you through setup and arms your first account.
  4. Prefer a terminal? Run sh install.sh, then panic setup.
  5. Run panic checkup for a read-only safety check. It changes nothing on your accounts.

One thing stays yours

You create your own access with each provider: a sign-in client for Gmail, a token for GitHub, an app registration for Microsoft. Nothing is shared with anyone else, and your secrets sit in a vault on your machine that needs both a passphrase and a separate key file to open. If you leave "Remember on this Mac" ticked in the app, anyone using your unlocked Mac can open it.
